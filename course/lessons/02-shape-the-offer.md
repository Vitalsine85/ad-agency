## Lesson review

“Five AI videos” tells the buyer what you make. It does not explain why the work matters to their business. The expert in the video reframes the service around the outcome a roofer wants: more booked work and fewer gaps in the crew’s schedule.

**[Deliverable]**

**[Outcome]**

Five finished AI video ads.

A small ad campaign designed to bring in roofing inquiries when the crew has room for more work.

The second version is still a proposal, not a guarantee. “Designed to” is honest; “will bring” would require evidence you do not have yet.

## Build the offer from five decisions

**Result**

Name the business outcome the buyer wants.

**Problem**

Connect the offer to a costly situation they already recognize.

**First-step risk**

Make the initial test small enough to evaluate without a large commitment.

**Delivery**

State a realistic time frame and exactly what is included.

**Next action**

End with one clear response or booking step.

Give Claude the niche thesis from Lesson 1 and ask it to include all five decisions:

**Prompt (copy this asset):**

Turn my service into a specific offer for [niche]. Sell the business result, name the biggest costly problem, lower the risk of the first step, add a believable delivery time and a first-step bonus, then end with one explicit call to action. Keep every promise measurable and do not invent proof, guarantees, or client results.

## Audit the draft line by line

The video checks its roofing offer against the same five points: new customers, idle crew time, payment after delivery, a fast discounted test, and a specific next action.


The generated roofing offer checked against the five-part structure.

Underline the phrase that performs each job in your own draft. If a section exists only in your intention and not in the copy, rewrite it.

The source mentions figures such as $1,000 per video and a 24-hour “$500 instead of $5,000” test. Those are examples from the video, not validated market prices. Set a price only after you understand your costs and can give a truthful reason for any introductory discount.

Do not manufacture scarcity, testimonials, results, or guarantees. A modest, truthful offer is safer — and more useful — than persuasive copy built on facts you cannot support.
